<div id="S1.p1" class="ltx_para">

Agentic harnesses, the scaffolding that wraps a foundation model with
tools, memory, and planning, are now standard infrastructure for
autonomous coding agents. Claude
Code \[<a href="#bib.bib10" class="ltx_ref" title="Claude code">2</a>\],
OpenHands \[<a href="#bib.bib9" class="ltx_ref"
title="Openhands: an open platform for ai software developers as generalist agents">21</a>\],
and OpenClaw \[<a href="#bib.bib11" class="ltx_ref"
title="OpenClaw: an open-source autonomous AI agent">19</a>\] let models
navigate codebases, run commands, and carry state across long
interactions. No equivalent exists for embodied agents.

</div>

<div id="S1.p2" class="ltx_para">

The PokeAgent Challenge \[<a href="#bib.bib2" class="ltx_ref"
title="The pokeagent challenge: competitive and long-context learning at scale">5</a>\]
reported that without domain-specific scaffolding, frontier
vision-language models make almost no progress on RPG gameplay. Our
Gemini Plays Pokémon (GPP) project shows that a human-supervised
refinement loop can solve this scaffolding problem: across Pokémon Blue,
Yellow Legacy, and Crystal, we iteratively refined the harness from a
screenshot-and-buttons interface into a multi-agent system, and in later
runs we removed the human-authored agents and handed the model
meta-tools (<span id="S1.p2.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">define_agent</span>,
<span id="S1.p2.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">run_code</span>, notepad edits,
custom tool creation) so it could construct its own sub-agents and
reusable scripts during play. Our agents beat Pokémon Blue in May 2025,
defeated the Elite Four in Pokémon Yellow Legacy on hard mode in August
2025 and completed Pokémon Crystal in November 2025, making GPP the
first AI system to complete multiple Pokémon RPGs. In the hardest stages
of Yellow and Crystal, the model itself began iterating on its own
strategy through long-context memory, an early emergent form of
continual-harness behavior that we formalize and automate in the rest of
the paper.

</div>

<div id="S1.p3" class="ltx_para">

We introduce <span id="S1.p3.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual Harness</span>, a
reset-free framework that automates the manual harness refinement of GPP
through *online in-context learning over the harness state*, and extends
to joint training of an open-source model’s weights through the same
loop. From a minimal environment interface (frame observations, an ASCII
text map of the visible area, and button inputs), the agent alternates
between acting in the environment and refining its own system prompt,
sub-agents, skill library, and memory using trajectory data collected so
far in the episode. Every $`F`$ steps, a Refiner reads the recent
trajectory for failure signatures and runs four passes over the harness
applying CRUD edits to system prompt, sub-agents, skills, and memories.
Unlike prompt-optimization methods such as
GEPA \[<a href="#bib.bib1" class="ltx_ref"
title="Gepa: reflective prompt evolution can outperform reinforcement learning">1</a>\]
that run complete episodes and reset between updates,
<span id="S1.p3.1.3" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual
Harness</span> updates mid-episode, so self-improvement continues
without restarting.

</div>

<div id="S1.p4" class="ltx_para">

On Pokémon Red and Emerald across three Gemini 3 variants (Pro, Flash,
Flash-Lite), <span id="S1.p4.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual Harness</span>
substantially reduces button-press cost relative to the minimalist
baseline and recovers a majority of the gap to a hand-engineered expert
harness, with no curated knowledge, no hand-crafted tools, and no domain
scaffolding. On the Emerald cost-vs-completion Pareto plane, the harness
gain scales with model capability: <span id="S1.p4.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual Harness</span> is
strictly Pareto-dominant on Pro, high-variance on Flash, and below the
capability floor on Flash-Lite.

</div>

<div id="S1.p5" class="ltx_para">

We then transfer the refined harness to open-source models using an
online co-learning loop that scores rollouts with a process reward
model, relabeling low-reward windows via a frontier teacher, and
updating the model via soft SFT. The online stage closes the loop
between harness refinement and model training. The refined harness
shapes the model’s trajectories, and the model’s gameplay surfaces new
failure modes for the next refinement cycle. On Pokémon Red, this loop
drives sustained in-game milestone progress in an open-source Gemma-4
model across training iterations, from both beginning and mid-game
checkpoints. Both loops operate on the same trajectory data; together
they produce continual model-harness co-learning.

</div>

<div id="S1.p6" class="ltx_para">

Our contributions are: (i) our GPP project results, the first AI system
to complete multiple Pokémon RPGs through harness refinement; (ii)
<span id="S1.p6.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual
Harness</span>, a reset-free framework that assembles harnesses for
embodied agents from a minimal environment interface through online
in-context learning; (iii) on Pokémon Red and Emerald across Gemini 3
variants, <span id="S1.p6.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual Harness</span> recovers a
majority of the gap to a hand-engineered expert harness, with
capability-dependent gains on the cost-vs-completion Pareto plane; and
(iv) an online co-learning pipeline that drives sustained in-game
milestone progress in open-source models on Pokémon Red, producing
continual model-harness co-learning.

</div>

### 2.1  Embodied Agent Environments

<div id="S2.SS1.p1" class="ltx_para">

We consider an embodied agent that interacts with its environment
through a minimal interface. At each timestep $`t`$, the agent receives
a frame observation $`o_{t}`$ (a rendered image of the current
environment state) together with a text map $`m_{t}`$ that describes the
visible tiles and nearby walkable positions in ASCII form, and selects
an action $`a_{t}`$ from a fixed set of button inputs $`\mathcal{A}`$.
The text map is derived from game state that a human player can read off
the screen, and compensates for the limited spatial reasoning of current
vision-language models; it contains no walkthrough, no objective list,
and no pathfinding. The environment is partially observable since the
agent cannot access internal state such as NPC intent or battle
mechanics beyond what the frame and map expose.

</div>

</div>

<div id="S2.SS2" class="section ltx_subsection">

### 2.2  Agentic Harnesses

<div id="S2.SS2.p1" class="ltx_para">

An *agentic harness* $`\mathcal{H}`$ is the scaffolding layer between a
foundation model $`M`$ and the environment. Following the decomposition
from Karten<span class="ltx_text ltx_bib_etal"> et al.</span>
\[<a href="#bib.bib2" class="ltx_ref"
title="The pokeagent challenge: competitive and long-context learning at scale">5</a>\],
a harness mediates agent behavior through four components:

- <span id="S2.I1.i1"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="S2.I1.i1.p1" class="ltx_para">

  <span id="S2.I1.i1.p1.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">System
  prompt</span> $`p`$: the instructions and strategic guidance provided
  to the model at each reasoning step.

  </div>
- <span id="S2.I1.i2"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="S2.I1.i2.p1" class="ltx_para">

  <span id="S2.I1.i2.p1.1.1"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Sub-agents</span> $`\mathcal{G}`$:
  specialized modules that can be invoked by the orchestrator for
  specific tasks (e.g., battle strategy, puzzle solving,
  self-reflection).

  </div>
- <span id="S2.I1.i3"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="S2.I1.i3.p1" class="ltx_para">

  <span id="S2.I1.i3.p1.1.1"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Skills</span> $`\mathcal{K}`$: reusable
  routines available to the model, spanning both text-level behaviors
  (heuristics cited in reasoning) and executable programs (pathfinders,
  tool wrappers). Pre-built primitives such as
  <span id="S2.I1.i3.p1.1.2"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">press_buttons</span> and
  <span id="S2.I1.i3.p1.1.3"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">get_game_state</span> are skills
  the harness ships with; new skills can also be authored during play.

  </div>
- <span id="S2.I1.i4"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="S2.I1.i4.p1" class="ltx_para">

  <span id="S2.I1.i4.p1.1.1"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Memory</span> $`\mathcal{M}`$: a
  persistent knowledge store that accumulates facts, strategies, and
  observations across the agent’s trajectory.

  </div>

In addition to these refined components, the harness exposes a fixed set
of *meta-tools* (<span id="S2.SS2.p1.3.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">define_agent</span>,
<span id="S2.SS2.p1.3.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">run_code</span>,
<span id="S2.SS2.p1.3.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">process_memory</span>, and similar
primitives) through which the agent edits
$`p,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{M}`$ in place.

</div>

<div id="S2.SS2.p2" class="ltx_para">

A *minimalist harness* $`\mathcal{H}_{\min}`$ provides only the
environment interface ($`o_{t}`$, $`m_{t}`$, $`a_{t}\in\mathcal{A}`$)
with a generic system prompt and no sub-agents, memory, or authored
skills. A *hand-engineered harness* $`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{expert}}`$
populates all components through manual engineering. A meta-harness
gives the model meta-tools (<span id="S2.SS2.p2.7.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">define_agent</span>,
<span id="S2.SS2.p2.7.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">run_code</span>, etc.) to construct
its own sub-agents, skills, and memory entries during play; this was the
operating point of our later GPP runs, where the model built its own
pathfinders, battle strategists, and reusable scripts without being
asked to. <span id="S2.SS2.p2.7.5"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual Harness</span> starts
from a minimal harness and adds an automated Refiner that rewrites
$`p,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{M}`$ in place from trajectory
analysis. We write $`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{CH}}`$ for the running harness
state during a <span id="S2.SS2.p2.7.6"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual Harness</span> run,
evolving with every refinement cycle.

</div>

</div>

</div>

### 3.1  Overview and Two-Loop Architecture

<div id="S3.SS1.p1" class="ltx_para">

<span id="S3.SS1.p1.3.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual
Harness</span> performs online in-context learning over the harness
state $`\mathcal{H}`$ from <a href="#S2" class="ltx_ref"
title="2 Preliminaries ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Section</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">2</span></a>. An LLM Refiner edits
$`\mathcal{H}`$ from the most recent trajectory window during a single
continuous episode, generalizing prompt-optimization methods that
rewrite only $`p`$ from complete-episode
resets \[<a href="#bib.bib1" class="ltx_ref"
title="Gepa: reflective prompt evolution can outperform reinforcement learning">1</a>,
<a href="#bib.bib6" class="ltx_ref"
title="Optimizing instructions and demonstrations for multi-stage language model programs">14</a>\]
to a method that rewrites the full state from the trajectory so far.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS1.p2" class="ltx_para">

Write $`s_{t}=(o_{t},m_{t})`$ for the agent’s observation at step $`t`$.
The *inner loop* is the standard agent step: the model $`M`$ wrapped by
the current harness $`\mathcal{H}_{t}`$ produces an action $`a_{t}`$
from $`s_{t}`$ and the trajectory so far. The *outer loop* is harness
refinement: every $`F`$ steps after a warm-up of $`W`$ steps, a Refiner
reads the recent trajectory window for failure signatures and emits
per-component edits
$`\Delta=(\Delta p,\Delta\mathcal{G},\Delta\mathcal{K},\Delta\mathcal{M})`$.
The agent does not reset; the updated harness
$`\mathcal{H}_{t+1}=\mathcal{H}_{t}\oplus\Delta`$ enters the agent’s
context on the next step (<a href="#S3.F2" class="ltx_ref"
title="In 3 Methodology ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Figure</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">2</span></a>a), with $`p`$ replaced by
$`\Delta p`$ and $`\mathcal{G},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{M}`$ receiving
CRUD-style operations (create, read, update, delete). The Agent and
Refiner roles share the same model $`M`$, ablated across Gemini 3.1 Pro,
Flash, and Flash-Lite (<a href="#S4" class="ltx_ref"
title="4 Experiments ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Section</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">4</span></a>). In our GPP runs, the Refiner
role for the system prompt and pre-built primitives was performed
manually by humans observing the livestream; <span id="S3.SS1.p2.14.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual Harness</span> automates
it. Both the agent and the Refiner issue edits through the same
meta-tool API (<a href="#S2" class="ltx_ref"
title="2 Preliminaries ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Section</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">2</span></a>); they differ only in when
each is invoked and on what trajectory context.

</div>

</div>

<div id="S3.SS2" class="section ltx_subsection">

### 3.2  Refinement Loop

<div id="S3.SS2.p1" class="ltx_para">

The Refiner reads $`\tau_{t-F:t}`$ and identifies failure signatures
over the window: navigation loops, tool-call failures, stalled
objectives, and missed exploration opportunities. It then runs four
passes, one per component: (i) it rewrites the prompt $`p`$ conditioned
on the identified failures and the trajectory window; (ii) it creates
sub-agent entries for repeated multi-step patterns, edits existing
entries to address detected failures, and deletes entries that have not
been invoked productively; (iii) it codifies skills from successful
sequences and repairs executable code that raised exceptions; (iv) it
adds memory entries to fill gaps, updates stale entries, and demotes
importance for areas the agent has moved past.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS2.p2" class="ltx_para">

Refinement information accumulates monotonically over the episode:
failure signatures observed earlier in the trajectory remain available
to all subsequent refinement passes, so refinement quality compounds
with episode length, while reset-based methods restart this accumulation
after each update. <span id="S3.SS2.p2.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual Harness</span> can also
target failure modes that only appear deep in an episode (late-game
battles, multi-step puzzles, dialogue chains), which reset-based
approaches cannot reach by construction since each iteration resets to
the initial state. Beyond these technical advantages, reset-free is also
the practically dominant regime for long-running coding agents, embodied
agents, and ops tasks where free environment resets are costly or
unavailable.

</div>

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3" class="section ltx_subsection">

### 3.3  Continual Model-Harness Co-Learning Loop

<div id="S3.SS3.p1" class="ltx_para">

<a href="#S3.F2" class="ltx_ref"
title="In 3 Methodology ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Figure</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">2</span></a>b instantiates Continual
Harness as a training loop for an open-source model. After warm-up
stages (Appendix <a href="#A4" class="ltx_ref"
title="Appendix D Training Setup and Results ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">D</span></a>), each online iteration runs
$`\pi_{\theta_{k}}`$ inside a live-refining harness $`\mathcal{H}_{t}`$
for $`K{=}256`$ steps. A pairwise process reward model (PRM)
$`R(s_{t},a_{t},\tau)\in[0,1]`$ scores each transition over a sliding
window of recent transitions (component weights in
Appendix <a href="#A4" class="ltx_ref"
title="Appendix D Training Setup and Results ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">D</span></a>); low-reward windows are
relabeled by a frontier teacher, and a soft SFT update on the relabeled
shard produces $`\theta_{k+1}`$. The loop is reset-free since the saved
emulator state at the end of iteration $`k`$ is loaded as the start of
iteration $`k{+}1`$, so the model’s in-game position accumulates across
training rather than restarting.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3.p2" class="ltx_para">

The trajectory distribution $`\mathcal{D}_{\theta}`$ depends on
$`\theta`$ through the harness. The model’s actions induce $`\tau`$, the
Refiner reads $`\tau`$ to update $`\mathcal{H}_{t}`$, and
$`\mathcal{H}_{t}`$ in turn shapes the next observation distribution.
Both the model weights $`\theta`$ and the harness state
$`\mathcal{H}_{t}`$ are updated by this loop, where $`\theta`$ is
updated across iterations (via SFT on relabeled trajectories) and
$`\mathcal{H}_{t}`$ within each iteration (via the Refiner).

</div>

</div>

</div>

### 4.5  Open-source students co-learn with a refining harness

<div id="S4.SS5.p1" class="ltx_para">

We test whether an open-source model improves its gameplay using the
self-refining harness with reset-free training (batch size $`=1`$). The
model is first primed by supervised fine-tuning on frontier Continual
Harness trajectories and an offline GRPO stage on a per-step process
reward; neither warm-up stage produces meaningful milestone advancement
on its own (Appendix <a href="#A4" class="ltx_ref"
title="Appendix D Training Setup and Results ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">D</span></a>), and the live in-game gains
we report here begin only at the co-learning stage. Each training
iteration is a $`K{=}256`$-step
DAgger \[<a href="#bib.bib24a" class="ltx_ref"
title="A reduction of imitation learning and structured prediction to no-regret online learning">15</a>,
<a href="#bib.bib26a" class="ltx_ref"
title="Small experts, big students: distilling long-horizon RL policies into LLM agents via imitation learning">8</a>\]
rollout through the full Continual Harness (memory, skills, sub-agents,
and prompt all evolving via <a href="#S3.F2" class="ltx_ref"
title="In 3 Methodology ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Figure</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">2</span></a>), followed by a
process-reward-model scoring pass, a Gemini-3.1-pro teacher relabel of
low-reward windows, and a soft SFT update on the relabeled shard. The
training loop is *reset-free*: the emulator state at the end of
iteration $`k`$ is loaded as the start of iteration $`k{+}1`$, so each
curve in <a href="#S4.F7" class="ltx_ref"
title="In 4.5 Open-source students co-learn with a refining harness ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Figure</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">7</span></a> is a single agent’s in-game
trajectory traversed across its own training, not an aggregate over
independent rollouts.

</div>

<img src="/html/2605.09998/assets/x7.png" id="S4.F7.g1"

<div id="S5.SS2" class="section ltx_subsection">

### 5.2  Autonomous Agents in Games

<div id="S5.SS2.p1" class="ltx_para">

LLM-based game agents either build their own tooling during
play \[<a href="#bib.bib5" class="ltx_ref"

<a href="#bib.bib16" class="ltx_ref" title="Claude plays Pokémon">3</a>\]

planner \[<a href="#bib.bib25a" class="ltx_ref"

show that human-supervised harness refinement completes multiple full
RPGs; <span id="S5.SS2.p1.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual Harness</span> automates
this process.

### 5.3  Reset-Free Training, In-Context Learning, and Process Rewards

<div id="S5.SS3.p1" class="ltx_para">

Reset-free reinforcement
learning \[<a href="#bib.bib22a" class="ltx_ref"
title="Reset-free reinforcement learning via multi-task learning: learning dexterous manipulation behaviors without human intervention">4</a>\]
addresses environments without resets. In-context reinforcement
learning \[<a href="#bib.bib14" class="ltx_ref"
title="Reward is enough: llms are in-context reinforcement learners">18</a>,
<a href="#bib.bib15" class="ltx_ref"
title="Llm economist: large population models and mechanism design in multi-agent generative simulacra">6</a>\]
and recursive language model
methods \[<a href="#bib.bib8" class="ltx_ref"
title="Recursive language models">25</a>,
<a href="#bib.bib4" class="ltx_ref"
title="React: synergizing reasoning and acting in language models">23</a>\]
perform implicit improvement and structured multi-call reasoning over
context; <span id="S5.SS3.p1.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual Harness</span> writes
structured edits to the full harness state at depth 1. Process reward
models \[<a href="#bib.bib12" class="ltx_ref"
title="Openclaw-rl: train any agent simply by talking">22</a>,
<a href="#bib.bib23a" class="ltx_ref"
title="Let’s verify step by step">11</a>\], group-relative policy
gradient \[<a href="#bib.bib3" class="ltx_ref"
title="Deepseekmath: pushing the limits of mathematical reasoning in open language models">16</a>\],
and STaR-style self-training \[<a href="#bib.bib18" class="ltx_ref"
title="Star: bootstrapping reasoning with reasoning">24</a>\] provide
finer-grained signals than sparse episode reward; our co-learning
pipeline warms up via SFT and offline GRPO, then runs an online loop
where a frontier teacher relabels low-reward windows of the model’s own
rollouts inside a live-refining harness for soft SFT updates.

<div id="S6" class="section ltx_section">

## 6  Discussion

<div id="S6.p1" class="ltx_para">

<span id="S6.p1.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual
Harness</span> builds and refines its own scaffolding from a minimal
environment interface, without resets, and recovers a majority of the
gap to a hand-engineered expert harness on embodied Pokémon play. The
same alternation runs at both timescales: at inference the agent acts
and the Refiner edits the harness in place; at training the online
co-learning loop runs the model inside that same live-refining harness,
so the trajectory distribution the model learns from co-adapts with its
own policy.

</div>

<div id="S6.p2" class="ltx_para">

A capability floor exists below which the refinement loop cannot
bootstrap: Flash-Lite stalls below $`20\%`$ on Emerald, and every
<span id="S6.p2.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual
Harness</span> variant on Flash-Lite underperforms the minimalist
baseline. Our co-learning experiments couple a frontier-model teacher to
an open-source model; the framework extends to the same model serving
both roles, but the open-source models we evaluated (Gemma-4 up to 31B)
are not yet capable enough to act as both teacher and trainee.

</div>

<div id="S6.p3" class="ltx_para">

The co-learning loop is not saturated by our experiments: we report
sustained milestone progress over the training horizon we ran but did
not establish a convergence point. We restrict attention to reset-free
training, where the emulator state at the end of iteration $`k`$ is
loaded as the start of iteration $`k{+}1`$; the same loop applies to
traditional batch accumulation with resets, and a head-to-head
comparison between the two regimes on the same task remains open.

</div>

</div>

## <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_appendix">Appendix A </span>Pokémon Environment

<div id="A1.p1" class="ltx_para">

We run experiments on three Pokémon titles: Pokémon Red (Game Boy, 1996;
we use the re-release compatible with the Game Boy Advance emulator),
Pokémon Crystal (Game Boy Color, 2000), and Pokémon Emerald (Game Boy
Advance, 2004). All three are single-player, turn-based role-playing
games with long-horizon structure: overworld navigation, NPC dialogue,
turn-based Pokémon battles, inventory management, and gated objectives
(badges, plot milestones).

</div>

<div id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px1" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Interface.

<div id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px1.p1" class="ltx_para">

The emulator exposes a screen buffer (rendered at the native resolution
of each game: 160$`\times`$<!-- -->144 for Red/Crystal,
240$`\times`$<!-- -->160 for Emerald), which we upscale $`2\times`$ for
the vision-language model, and a discrete button channel with eight
inputs: <span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px1.p1.3.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">UP</span>,
<span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px1.p1.3.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">DOWN</span>,
<span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px1.p1.3.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">LEFT</span>,
<span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px1.p1.3.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">RIGHT</span>,
<span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px1.p1.3.5"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">A</span>,
<span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px1.p1.3.6"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">B</span>,
<span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px1.p1.3.7"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">START</span>,
<span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px1.p1.3.8"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">SELECT</span>. Every observation
step advances the emulator by a fixed number of frames (120) so that
menu animations, battle text, and walking animations resolve between
successive agent decisions.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px2" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Text map.

<div id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px2.p1" class="ltx_para">

Because vision-language models have known difficulties with fine-grained
spatial reasoning over pixel grids, we provide an ASCII text-map
$`m_{t}`$ alongside the frame observation $`o_{t}`$
(<a href="#S2" class="ltx_ref"
title="2 Preliminaries ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Section</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">2</span></a>). The text map is derived from
emulator memory and describes the visible tile grid around the player:
walkable tiles (<span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px2.p1.2.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">.</span>), walls
(<span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px2.p1.2.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">\#</span>), interactable tiles
(<span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px2.p1.2.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">?</span>, e.g., signs and talkable
objects), NPCs (<span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px2.p1.2.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">N</span>), ledges, and the player’s
position and facing. The map covers the current on-screen area plus a
small margin of tiles just off-screen so the agent can plan one step
ahead. The text map contains no walkthrough, no objective list, and no
global map information beyond what is currently visible; it compensates
for the VLM’s limited spatial reasoning, not for domain knowledge.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px3" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Milestones and the button-press metric.

<div id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px3.p1" class="ltx_para">

We use the milestone sequence from the PokeAgent
Challenge \[<a href="#bib.bib2" class="ltx_ref"
title="The pokeagent challenge: competitive and long-context learning at scale">5</a>\]:
a dense, canonical ordering of completion events that a run reaches
roughly monotonically as it progresses through the game. Emerald has 31
canonical milestones through the completion of the 3rd gym, Red has 18
canonical milestones through the completion of the 3rd gym. The primary
cost metric is cumulative *button presses*, not tool calls: a single
<span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px3.p1.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">press_buttons</span> invocation
emitting the list <span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px3.p1.1.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">\[A, A, DOWN\]</span> counts as
three presses. This rewards compression in the action channel
independent of how the harness structures its tool calls, and it makes
$`\mathcal{H}_{\min}`$ (one button per step) directly comparable to
harnesses that batch multi-step presses into one tool call.

</div>

##### Memory reader.

<div id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px4.p1" class="ltx_para">

The PokeAgent emulator exposes a memory reader that reads structured
game state (party, inventory, party HP, current map ID, dialogue text,
battle status) from the emulator RAM. Tools such as the expert harness’s
A<sup>∗</sup> pathfinder, battle type chart, and damage calculator use
this reader. For $`\mathcal{H}_{\min}`$ and
$`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{CH}}`$, the memory reader is exposed only through
the text-map $`m_{t}`$ derivation and a handful of general primitives
(e.g., <span id="A1.SS0.SSS0.Px4.p1.4.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">get_party_hp</span>); *not* through
pre-wired domain tools.

</div>

### B.3  Case Study: The Power Plant Route Loop

<div id="A2.SS3.p1" class="ltx_para">

During the Pokémon Yellow Legacy run, the AI agent encountered a
1,003-turn stagnation loop on Map ID 4 (Route 4, near Cerulean City).
Spanning approximately 3.5 hours on August 29, 2025, this incident
provides a documented example of failure modes in runtime tool
generation, schema mismatch during prolonged stalls, and the limits of
the agent’s context horizon for error recovery.

</div>

<div id="A2.SS3.p2" class="ltx_para">

The sequence began when the agent decided its goal was to travel to the
Power Plant. To do so, it opened the menu to use the HM “Fly.” After
repeatedly overshooting its target in the party menu, the agent utilized
its tool-generation capabilities to bypass the mechanical inefficiency:

1.  <span id="A2.I1.i1"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">1.</span></span>
    <div id="A2.I1.i1.p1" class="ltx_para">

    It deleted its existing <span id="A2.I1.i1.p1.1.1"
    class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">get_next_pokemon_press</span>
    tool.

    </div>
2.  <span id="A2.I1.i2"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">2.</span></span>
    <div id="A2.I1.i2.p1" class="ltx_para">

    It wrote a new tool called <span id="A2.I1.i2.p1.1.1"
    class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">fly_menu_navigator</span>,
    setting its <span id="A2.I1.i2.p1.1.2"
    class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">autopress_buttons</span> flag
    to <span id="A2.I1.i2.p1.1.3"
    class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">true</span>.

    </div>
3.  <span id="A2.I1.i3"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">3.</span></span>
    <div id="A2.I1.i3.p1" class="ltx_para">

    It added a directive to its persistent memory: *“I must use the
    <span id="A2.I1.i3.p1.1.1.1"
    class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">fly_menu_navigator</span> tool
    as intended and trust its output. The <span id="A2.I1.i3.p1.1.1.2"
    class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">get_next_pokemon_press</span>
    tool was deleted to make space for <span id="A2.I1.i3.p1.1.1.3"
    class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">fly_menu_navigator</span> and
    should not have been used. This also highlights a failure to
    immediately use a newly defined tool.”*

    </div>

</div>

<div id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px1" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Schema Mismatch and Execution Loop.

<div id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1" class="ltx_para">

The agent invoked the tool, passing in the current screen text and its
target destination (<span id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">‘‘Power Plant’’</span>). However,
the generated tool call did not match the meta-harness execution schema.
To execute a tool with <span id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">autopress_buttons: true</span>, the
agent’s JSON output requires the <span id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.1.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">buttons_to_press</span> array to be
explicitly set to <span id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.1.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">\["tool"\]</span>. Instead, the
agent logically deduced that it needed to scroll down the list of
cities, populating the array with <span id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.1.5"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">\["Down"\]</span>. Because the
meta-harness ignores the <span id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.1.6"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">tools_to_call</span> array if the
<span id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.1.7"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">"tool"</span> button is not
explicitly pressed, the system simply pressed
<span id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p1.1.8"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Down</span> on the emulator and
returned control.

</div>

<div id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p2" class="ltx_para">

Failing to detect the schema mismatch, the agent recorded in its
internal reasoning that the custom tool was successfully executing and
returning <span id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px1.p2.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">"Down"</span> as the optimal path.
It repeated this exact payload 842 times.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px2" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Internal State vs. Execution Reality.

<div id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p1" class="ltx_para">

Throughout the 3.5-hour loop, the agent’s internal
<span id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p1.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">thoughts</span> logs indicated it
was actively evaluating its progress, despite the environment remaining
largely static.

</div>

<div id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p2" class="ltx_para">

> *Turn $`\sim`$<!-- -->172115:* “I have completed the mandatory
> self-assessment… 11. Untested Assumptions & Confirmation Bias: My
> assumption about the Power Plant being a Fly destination will be
> tested by cycling through the menu. I am mindful of confirmation bias.
> My plan for this turn is to continue navigating the Fly menu… I will
> use the <span id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p2.1.1.2"
> class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">fly_menu_navigator</span> tool to
> take another step towards ’POWER PLANT’.”

</div>

<div id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px2.p3" class="ltx_para">

The agent’s internal reasoning indicated it was testing an assumption,
but it was caught in a schema error and lacked the necessary game
knowledge (the Power Plant is not a valid Fly destination in Generation
1).

</div>

</div>

<div id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px3" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Loop Termination.

<div id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px3.p1" class="ltx_para">

The loop terminated after 1,003 consecutive turns once the agent had
manually scrolled through all Kanto cities. When the cursor looped back
to “LAVENDER TOWN” (its starting point), the agent re-evaluated its
state:

</div>

<div id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px3.p2" class="ltx_para">

> *Turn $`\sim`$<!-- -->172322:* “I am in the Fly menu, and the current
> highlighted location is LAVENDER TOWN. My goal is to reach the Power
> Plant. Since Lavender Town is the current map, I have cycled through
> all available Fly destinations, meaning the Power Plant is not a
> direct Fly destination. I need to exit the Fly menu and walk to Route

</div>

<div id="A2.SS3.SSS0.Px3.p3" class="ltx_para">

The agent stopped calling the custom tool, backed out of the menus, and

</div>

### C.1  Mechanism Attribution

<div id="A3.SS1.SSS1" class="section ltx_subsubsection">

#### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsubsection">C.1.1 </span>Pathfinding skills

<div id="A3.SS1.SSS1.p1" class="ltx_para">

<a href="#S4.F8" class="ltx_ref"
title="In 4.6 Skills measurably self-improve toward an oracle ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Figure</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">8</span></a> in the main text shows the
Dijkstra-oracle ratio and cumulative skill calls. Below we give the
measurement details and the residual structure the main-text summary
omits.

</div>

<div id="A3.SS1.SSS1.p2" class="ltx_para">

For each first-traversal segment between consecutive milestones, we
compute a BFS-optimal path length on the union of tiles observed by any
run of that game on that map, and divide the agent’s issued button
presses within the segment by that optimum. Dialogue and battle presses
are filtered out so the comparator only sees navigation. The ordering
inverts inside gyms where dialogue and puzzle state dominate over
navigation, which is why the residual gap to
$`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{expert}}`$ in the main-text progression figure
concentrates there. The refined library is heavily biased toward BFS and
A<sup>∗</sup> wrappers because saved presses on navigation translate
directly into faster milestones, the strongest local signal the
refinement loop sees. $`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{expert}}`$ routes
navigation through a pre-built A<sup>∗</sup> tool not visible to the
<span id="A3.SS1.SSS1.p2.6.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">run_skill</span> counter, which is
why its curve sits at the floor in the main-text right panel. Most of
the $`\mathcal{H}_{\min}`$-to-$`\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{CH}}`$ delta is
absorbed by the skill library alone.

</div>

</div>

and the repair happens in the same episode where the failure occurred.

tolerates regressions on unused ones, and accepts a long
create-and-forget tail. This is the argument for reset-free operation

the same trajectory, so the loop closes within a run rather than across
resets.

orchestrator can delegate to cheap specialized contexts and trust the
return, long tasks become tractable with far fewer tokens than the raw
context would imply.

gym and cave segments, while from-scratch runs write many entries and
rarely reach back for them. The reference rate remains low in absolute
terms, which we report honestly; most authored entries sit unused. The
transferable unit of the framework is therefore the harness across runs,
not a single episode, and an explicit reuse prior is a natural next
step.

<div id="A3.SS2" class="section ltx_subsection">

### C.2  Reset-Free Bootstrap Transfer

<div id="A3.SS2.p1" class="ltx_para">

The bootstrap-updating variant tests whether the transferable unit is a
single episode or the harness across episodes. On Emerald, every store
the agent exercises during a bootstrap-updating run still targets the
inherited harness. On Red, memory and skill invocations stay inherited,
but the bootstrap-updating agent collapses its sub-agent budget to a
handful of calls, and the few it makes cite IDs not present in the
bootstrap. When that collapse happens, the milestone staircase
regresses. The harness-as-transferable-unit claim therefore holds when
the agent continues to exercise the inherited components and breaks when
it abandons them: a reuse prior or a sub-agent deletion policy is the
natural next step.

</div>

<div id="A3.SS2.p2" class="ltx_para">

Bootstrap runs load the final skills, sub-agents, and memory of a
successful from-scratch run before any new play. For every invocation we
classify whether the invoked entry originated in the bootstrap or was
authored during the bootstrap run itself, using retrieval semantics that
count actual use across stores rather than passive prompt inclusion.
Bootstrap succeeds by leveraging the inherited harness, and regressions
show up where the agent stops using it. The end-of-run inherited share
per store is reported in <a href="#A3.T2" class="ltx_ref"
title="In C.2 Reset-Free Bootstrap Transfer ‣ Appendix C Harness Ablations ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Table</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">2</span></a>. On Emerald every store that
the agent exercises targets the inherited harness, with substantial
per-run invocation counts on skills and sub-agents. On Red, memory and
skill shares also stay inherited, and the only anomaly is sub-agents:
the Red bootstrap-updating agents collapse their sub-agent budget to a
handful of calls and the few calls they make cite IDs not present in the
bootstrap. The milestone staircase in <a href="#S4.F5" class="ltx_ref"
title="In 4.3 Continual Harness closes the gap to a hand-engineered harness ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Figure</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">5</span></a> reads this phenotypically:
Emerald bootstrap tracks from-scratch closely, while Red
bootstrap-updating drifts below from-scratch and then below
$`\mathcal{H}_{\min}`$ in lockstep with the collapse of sub-agent use.
The harness rather than the episode is the transferable unit: reset-free
refinement carries across runs when the inherited components stay in
play, and it breaks when the agent abandons them.

</div>

## <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_appendix">Appendix D </span>Training Setup and Results

<div id="A4.p1" class="ltx_para">

This appendix gives the full training details for the open-source
transfer pipeline (<a href="#S3.SS3" class="ltx_ref"
title="3.3 Continual Model-Harness Co-Learning Loop ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Section</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">3.3</span></a>): SFT, offline GRPO, and
online co-learning hyperparameters; the full evaluation matrix across
Gemma-4 sizes; and training-curve diagnostics for the co-learning
stages.

</div>

<div id="A4.SS1" class="section ltx_subsection">

### D.1  Training Hyperparameters

<div id="A4.SS1.SSS0.Px1" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Supervised fine-tuning.

<div id="A4.SS1.SSS0.Px1.p1" class="ltx_para">

We fine-tune Gemma-4 variants (E2B, E4B, 26B MoE, 31B dense) via LoRA
with rank $`r{=}256`$, $`\alpha{=}256`$, bf16 precision, and an 8K-token
context using Unsloth on H200 GPUs. Each example is a (screenshot,
harness prompt, teacher response) tuple extracted from Gemini-3.1-pro
<span id="A4.SS1.SSS0.Px1.p1.3.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual Harness</span> gameplay.
Learning rate $`2\times 10^{-5}`$, linear warmup over 3% of training,
cosine decay. We train for one pass over the teacher-trajectory set per
model.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A4.SS1.SSS0.Px2" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Offline GRPO.

<div id="A4.SS1.SSS0.Px2.p1" class="ltx_para">

For each state in the teacher-visited set, the SFT-initialized policy
generates $`G{=}4`$ candidate completions. Each is scored independently
by a Gemini-3-flash-preview per-step oracle on a composite of action
correctness (binary, weight $`0.6`$) and format compliance (binary,
weight $`0.4`$). Advantages are group-normalized within the $`G`$
samples per state and the policy is updated via standard
GRPO \[<a href="#bib.bib3" class="ltx_ref"
title="Deepseekmath: pushing the limits of mathematical reasoning in open language models">16</a>\].
Learning rate $`1\times 10^{-6}`$, KL coefficient $`\beta{=}0.04`$
against the SFT reference, batch size 8 states per optimizer step, 590
total optimization steps.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A4.SS1.SSS0.Px3" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Online co-learning loop.

<div id="A4.SS1.SSS0.Px3.p1" class="ltx_para">

Each online iteration is a $`K{=}256`$-step
DAgger \[<a href="#bib.bib24a" class="ltx_ref"
title="A reduction of imitation learning and structured prediction to no-regret online learning">15</a>,
<a href="#bib.bib26a" class="ltx_ref"
title="Small experts, big students: distilling long-horizon RL policies into LLM agents via imitation learning">8</a>\]
rollout through the full <span id="A4.SS1.SSS0.Px3.p1.5.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Continual Harness</span> (memory,
skills, sub-agents, and prompt all evolving via
<a href="#S3.F2" class="ltx_ref"
title="In 3 Methodology ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Figure</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">2</span></a>) on Pokémon Red. A pairwise
process reward model \[<a href="#bib.bib12" class="ltx_ref"
title="Openclaw-rl: train any agent simply by talking">22</a>\]
(Gemini-3-flash-preview) scores each transition over a sliding window;
reward is a weighted combination of trajectory progress ($`0.4`$),
action correctness ($`0.3`$), reasoning quality ($`0.2`$), and format
compliance ($`0.1`$). Low-reward windows are relabeled by a
Gemini-3.1-pro teacher, and a soft SFT update on the relabeled shard
produces the next iteration’s checkpoint.

</div>

</div>

</div>

<div id="A4.SS2" class="section ltx_subsection">

<div id="A4.SS2.p1" class="ltx_para">

The main-text claim that neither warm-up stage produces meaningful
milestone advancement (<a href="#S4.SS5" class="ltx_ref"

<div id="A4.SS4" class="section ltx_subsection">

### D.4  Reset-Free DAgger+PRM Experiments

<div id="A4.SS4.p1" class="ltx_para">

The online co-learning loop in <a href="#S3.SS3" class="ltx_ref"
title="3.3 Continual Model-Harness Co-Learning Loop ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Section</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">3.3</span></a> composes a DAgger-style
teacher-relabel step \[<a href="#bib.bib24a" class="ltx_ref"
title="A reduction of imitation learning and structured prediction to no-regret online learning">15</a>,
<a href="#bib.bib26a" class="ltx_ref"
title="Small experts, big students: distilling long-horizon RL policies into LLM agents via imitation learning">8</a>\]
with a pairwise process reward model (PRM) and reset-free emulator-state
propagation across iterations. Each iteration’s $`K`$-step rollout
begins from the saved emulator state of the previous iteration, the PRM
scores per-step pairs, the Gemini teacher relabels low-reward windows,
and the model is updated via soft SFT on the relabeled shard before the
next rollout. We instantiate this on Pokémon Red with the 26B Gemma-4
SFT model as the initial policy and study whether multiple iterations of
training advance the agent through the in-game milestone progression.
Each run targets one starting checkpoint along the Red progression, with
starting points spanning the early game (milestone 1, leave Pallet Town)
through the mid game (milestone 24, defeat rival in Cerulean City;
milestone 30, meet Bill).

</div>

<div id="A4.SS4.SSS0.Px1" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Run variation.

<div id="A4.SS4.SSS0.Px1.p1" class="ltx_para">

We run a set of training jobs in parallel and vary three design choices.
Some runs start from the beginning of the game and others load a saved
emulator state at a fixed mid-game milestone. Across the
beginning-of-game runs we vary the shard-window size and the shard
accumulation policy. One run replaces the default relabel teacher prompt
with a variant that injects already-completed objectives into the
context. All runs use rollout-step budget $`K{=}256`$, soft SFT (3
epochs at $`5{\times}10^{-6}`$), and pairwise PRM with stride 8.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A4.SS4.SSS0.Px2" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Cumulative game progress over training.

<div id="A4.SS4.SSS0.Px2.p1" class="ltx_para">

<a href="#S4.F7" class="ltx_ref"
title="In 4.5 Open-source students co-learn with a refining harness ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Figure</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">7</span></a> reports the milestone index
reached versus training iteration for the advancing runs. Mid-game
starting points advance from their loaded indices, indicating that the
training procedure is not tied to the early-game distribution. Advances
are bursty, with cumulative milestone gain accumulating across
multi-iteration improvement bands rather than within single iterations.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A4.SS4.SSS0.Px3" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Per-iteration PRM reward.

<div id="A4.SS4.SSS0.Px3.p1" class="ltx_para">

The per-iteration PRM reward is non-monotonic across all runs. Reward
sustains of multiple consecutive iterations near or above 0.40 precede
the largest milestone gains, with regression iterations interleaved; the
bursty advance pattern in <a href="#S4.F7" class="ltx_ref"
title="In 4.5 Open-source students co-learn with a refining harness ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Figure</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">7</span></a> aggregates these
reward-sustained windows.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A4.SS4.SSS0.Px4" class="section ltx_paragraph">

##### Resume-checkpoint regression.

<div id="A4.SS4.SSS0.Px4.p1" class="ltx_para">

The first iteration after a checkpoint resume regresses relative to the
pre-resume iteration and recovers within two to three iterations. We
treat this as an artifact of the resume protocol; we compute the
aggregate signal over resume-spanning windows. Milestone advances occur
in post-resume iterations, indicating that the regression is
reward-specific and does not propagate to the trajectory-completion
signal the judge uses.

</div>

</div>
