<div class="ltx_abstract">

###### Abstract

This beta technical report asks how reusable experience should be
represented so that it can function as effective test-time control and
as a substrate for iterative evolution. We study this question in 4,590
controlled trials across 45 scientific code-solving scenarios. We find
that documentation-oriented Skill packages provide unstable control:
their useful signal is sparse, and expanding a compact experience object
into a fuller documentation package often fails to help and can degrade
the overall average. We further show that representation itself is a
first-order factor. A compact Gene representation yields the strongest
overall average, remains competitive under substantial structural
perturbations, and outperforms matched-budget Skill fragments, while
reattaching documentation-oriented material usually weakens rather than
improves it. Beyond one-shot control, we show that Gene is also a better
carrier for iterative experience accumulation: attached failure history
is more effective in Gene than in Skill or freeform text, editable
structure matters beyond content alone, and failure information is most
useful when distilled into compact warnings rather than naively
appended. On CritPt, gene-evolved systems improve over their paired base
models from 9.1% to 18.57% and from 17.7% to 27.14%. These results
suggest that the core problem in experience reuse is not how to supply
more experience, but how to encode experience as a compact,
control-oriented, evolution-ready object.

</div>

## <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_section">1 </span>Introduction

<div id="S1.p1" class="ltx_para">

Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly designed to improve
through the accumulation and reuse of prior experience. Unlike one-shot
prompt engineering, a growing body of work equips agents with mechanisms
such as reflection, reusable experience repositories, and structured
skill artifacts to preserve useful information from past interactions
and reuse it in subsequent problem
solving \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">auto-agent-survey</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">LLM-based-agent</span>\].
Such experience may take the form of verbal reflection, external memory,
executable skills, or experience
libraries \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">Voyager</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">Reflexion</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">ExpeL</span>\].
Although these approaches differ in implementation, most of them treat
experience primarily as a content object that can be stored, retrieved,
and replayed. In contrast, this paper further asks whether such
experience can in fact function as a stable and effective control signal
at test time.

</div>

<div id="S1.p2" class="ltx_para">

This assumption is further reinforced by recent skill-style approaches.
Rather than preserving experience as free-form textual feedback or
isolated case fragments, these methods tend to organize experience into
procedural units with explicit interfaces, applicability conditions, and
execution structure. ProcMEM formulates experience as executable skills
with explicit execution
structure \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">ProcMEM</span>\],
while MemSkill further treats memory operations themselves as learnable
and evolvable
skills \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">MemSkill</span>\].
Memento-Skills \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">Memento-Skills</span>\]
and
CoWork-X \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">CoWork-X</span>\]
push this trend further by organizing experience as persistent,
structured, and compositional skill libraries to support reliable and
budget-aware improvement. Taken together, these studies reinforce a
stronger assumption about reusable experience: more complete and
structured experience representations should be more beneficial for
subsequent task solving.

</div>

<div id="S1.p3" class="ltx_para">

Our point of departure is to test this assumption directly. As shown
in <a href="#S1.F1" class="ltx_ref"
title="In 1 Introduction ‣ From Procedural Skills to Strategy Genes: Towards Experience-Driven Test-Time Evolution"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Figure</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">1</span></a>(b), a representative
comparison already suggests that more experience content does not
necessarily translate into better test-time control. A compact
<span id="S1.p3.4.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span>
representation, at roughly $`230`$ tokens, improves performance by
$`+3.0`$pp over the baseline, whereas a documentation-heavy
<span id="S1.p3.4.2" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span>
package, at roughly $`2{,}500`$ tokens, reduces performance by
$`-1.1`$pp. This contrast shifts attention from experience quantity to
experience representation: *what form of reusable experience can serve
as an effective and evolvable test-time control signal for LLM-based
task solving?*

</div>

<div id="S1.p4" class="ltx_para">

Building on this observation, this paper reexamines the experience
object itself. We treat a <span id="S1.p4.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">procedural skill</span> as a
documentation-oriented experience object that is useful for human
reading, instruction, and review, but not necessarily well suited to
serve as a directly usable control signal under limited inference
budget. In contrast, the <span id="S1.p4.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">strategy gene</span> proposed in this
work is a compact, structured, and behavior-oriented representation of
experience. Its objective is not documentary completeness, but higher
signal density, clearer applicability boundaries, and stronger control
relevance within a constrained token budget. Accordingly, a strategy
gene is not merely a compressed version of a procedural skill, but a
different abstraction of reusable experience. To support stable
accumulation and iterative revision of such objects, we introduce the
Gene Evolution Protocol (GEP) as a protocol layer for canonicalizing
genes into structured, evolvable experience representations.

</div>

<div id="S1.p5" class="ltx_para">

To study this object-level shift, we conduct 4,590 controlled
experiments on complex scientific tasks, spanning $`45`$ scenarios, and
design three analytical probes. The <span id="S1.p5.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Skill Probe</span> examines why
procedural skills often fail to provide stable and effective test-time
control. The <span id="S1.p5.1.2" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Gene
Probe</span> investigates why strategy genes can support
experience-based control with lower budget and greater robustness. The
<span id="S1.p5.1.3" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Evolution
Probe</span> asks which properties make strategy genes suitable as the
substrate for continual accumulation and experience-driven evolution.
Taken together, these probes are not intended to compare a set of prompt
tricks in isolation, but to systematically test a representational shift
from *documentation-oriented experience objects* to *control-oriented,
evolution-ready experience objects*.

</div>

<div id="S1.p6" class="ltx_para">

Our main findings are as follows. (1) *Procedural skills* are often
poorly aligned with the requirements of test-time control: only a narrow
subset of high-density procedural content contributes meaningfully,
while much of the surrounding documentation imposes additional burden.
(2) Representation is itself a first-order factor. Even when the
underlying experience content is held broadly constant, substantial
differences still arise from how that experience is packaged,
structured, and exposed to the model. (3) *Strategy genes* provide a
more token-efficient and control-oriented experience representation than
full skill-style documents, suggesting that effective reusable
experience is not equivalent to longer instruction. (4) Experience
accumulation is most effective when it remains selective and structured:
compact warnings, editable schema, and stable carriers are more useful
than additive attachment of diffuse history.

</div>

<div id="S1.p7" class="ltx_para">

The contributions of this paper are threefold:

1.  <span id="S1.I1.i1"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">1.</span></span>
    <div id="S1.I1.i1.p1" class="ltx_para">

    We recast reusable experience from a problem of storing and invoking
    content into one of representing test-time control signals.

    </div>
2.  <span id="S1.I1.i2"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">2.</span></span>
    <div id="S1.I1.i2.p1" class="ltx_para">

    Through controlled probes, we identify several object-level factors
    governing effective experience reuse, including information overload
    in documentation-oriented skills, representational packaging
    effects, structural robustness, bounded reuse, and selective
    experience accumulation.

    </div>
3.  <span id="S1.I1.i3"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">3.</span></span>
    <div id="S1.I1.i3.p1" class="ltx_para">

    We introduce strategy genes, organized under the Gene Evolution
    Protocol (GEP), as a protocolized control representation for
    reusable experience, and show in scientific code-solving settings
    that they provide a stronger interface than documentation-oriented
    skill objects for test-time control and iterative evolution.

    </div>

</div>

</div>

<div id="S2" class="section ltx_section">

## <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_section">2 </span>Related Work

<div id="S2.SS1" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">2.1 </span>Experience Reuse in Agents and Test-Time Improvement

<div id="S2.SS1.p1" class="ltx_para">

A growing body of work improves LLM agents by enabling them to
accumulate and reuse experience across tasks. One line of research
focuses on test-time refinement through critique, repair, and
reflection, where models iteratively improve outputs using
self-generated, execution-grounded, or tool-assisted feedback rather
than parameter
updates \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">Self-Refine</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">CRITIC</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">Self-Debug</span>\].
Another line externalizes experience into persistent memory or reusable
repositories, such as episodic verbal memory, natural-language lessons,
and executable skill libraries that can be recalled in later
tasks \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">Reflexion</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">ExpeL</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">Voyager</span>\].
Although these approaches differ in mechanism, they largely share the
same assumption: useful experience can be preserved as a reusable
content object and later replayed to improve subsequent problem solving.
Our work is motivated by a prior question left open by this literature:
once experience is preserved, what representation should it take when it
re-enters the model at test time so that it functions as stable and
effective control rather than merely additional context?

</div>

</div>

<div id="S2.SS2" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">2.2 </span>Experience Representation, Control, and Evolution

<div id="S2.SS2.p1" class="ltx_para">

Recent work has begun to make reusable experience more structured,
compact, and maintainable. Procedural-memory and skill-centric
approaches increasingly encode experience as explicit skill units with
executable structure, learnable operations, or persistent external skill
files \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">Memp</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">ProcMEM</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">MemSkill</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">Memento-Skills</span>\].
Related systems further organize such units into structured and
compositional skill libraries under reliability and budget constraints,
or extract reusable expertise patterns from trajectories to support
continual
refinement \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">CoWork-X</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">AutoRefine</span>\].
A closely related line studies how externalized experience should be
maintained and evolved during deployment, emphasizing streaming updates,
joint optimization of extraction and management, and even meta-evolution
of memory
architectures \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">Evo-Memory</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">TAME</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">UMEM</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">MemEvolve</span>\].
Taken together, these works increasingly recognize that reusable
experience should be compact, structured, and amenable to continual
refinement. However, their primary focus remains on how experience can
be accumulated, maintained, or evolved once externalized. By contrast,
our focus is on a prior representational question: what kind of
experience object is most suitable for acting as a test-time control
signal in the first place? This shift leads us from
documentation-oriented experience objects toward compact, failure-aware,
and protocolized control representations.

</div>

</div>

</div>

<div id="S3" class="section ltx_section">

## <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_section">3 </span>Experience Representations for Test-Time Control

<div id="S3.p1" class="ltx_para">

We study reusable experience as an *experience representation*: an
externalized object derived from prior problem solving and reintroduced
at test time to influence model behavior. This perspective shifts the
focus from whether experience can be stored and recalled to what
representational form allows it to function as effective test-time
control. It also motivates a distinction between documentation-oriented
representations, optimized for human use, and control-oriented
representations, optimized for model-facing inference under limited
budget.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS1" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">3.1 </span>Reusable Experience Representations

<div id="S3.SS1.p1" class="ltx_para">

Let $`\mathcal{H}=\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}`$ denote a set of prior
problem-solving trajectories. We define a *reusable experience
representation* as an externalized object

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
r=\phi(\mathcal{H}),\qquad r\in\mathcal{R},
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(1)</span> |

where $`\phi`$ abstracts prior experience into a reusable representation
space $`\mathcal{R}`$.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS1.p2" class="ltx_para">

Given a new task input $`x`$, a fixed model with parameters $`\theta`$
produces

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
y\sim p_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x,r).
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(2)</span> |

A representation is control-relevant only if it induces a measurable
shift in test-time behavior:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
p_{\theta}(y\mid x,r)\neq p_{\theta}(y\mid x,\varnothing)
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(3)</span> |

for a non-negligible subset of tasks under the target distribution.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS1.p3" class="ltx_para">

This definition excludes three nearby cases. Reusable experience
representations are not parameter updates, since $`\theta`$ remains
fixed; not one-off prompt tricks, since they are abstracted from prior
experience and intended for reuse; and not mere conversational recall,
since their role is to provide task-relevant control rather than
preserve dialogue continuity. Accordingly, the central question is not
only how experience is stored or retrieved, but what representational
form allows prior experience to act as effective test-time control.

</div>

</div>

<div id="S3.SS2" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">3.2 </span>Procedural Skills

<div id="S3.SS2.p1" class="ltx_para">

A *procedural skill* is a documentation-oriented experience
representation that organizes prior problem-solving knowledge into a
human-readable and reusable
package \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">SkillsBench</span>,
<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">AgentSkills</span>\].
It is designed primarily for interpretation, instruction, review, and
archival, rather than for direct behavioral control under constrained
inference-time budget.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS2.p2" class="ltx_para">

Formally, we model a procedural skill as a document package

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
s=\{d_{\text{main}},d_{\text{aux}}\},
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(4)</span> |

where

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
d_{\text{main}}=\{\texttt{overview},\texttt{workflow},\texttt{pitfalls},\texttt{error\_handling}\}
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(5)</span> |

contains the core procedural description, and

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
d_{\text{aux}}=\{\texttt{api\_notes},\texttt{examples},\texttt{scripts}\}
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(6)</span> |

contains supplementary reference and execution-support material. This
structure captures the documentation-heavy form of skill-style
experience used in our experiments, where the instantiated
<span id="S3.SS2.p2.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span> representation is
approximately $`2{,}500`$ tokens long.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS2.p3" class="ltx_para">

Under this view, procedural skills are valuable because they externalize
experience in a stable and communicable form, making prior solutions
easier to document, teach, maintain, and transfer across tasks. However,
these advantages do not imply suitability for inference-time control.
Procedural skills are optimized for readability and knowledge transfer,
whereas test-time control requires compact, behaviorally targeted
guidance that remains effective under limited token budget and
constrained attention. A representation that is useful for human
understanding may therefore be inefficient or even misaligned when
placed in model context. Whether documentation-oriented skill packages
can serve as effective control representations is thus an empirical
question rather than a
premise \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">AGENTS.md</span>\].

</div>

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">3.3 </span>Strategy Genes and the GEP Protocol

<div id="S3.SS3.p1" class="ltx_para">

We define a *strategy gene* as a control-oriented experience
representation distilled from prior problem-solving experience. Unlike
procedural skills, which package experience for reading, explanation,
and review, a strategy gene is designed to deliver control-relevant
experience under constrained inference budget. Its objective is not
documentary completeness, but compactness, structural clarity,
behavioral targeting, and failure awareness. Accordingly, a strategy
gene is not a shortened skill, but a different abstraction of reusable
experience.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3.p2" class="ltx_para">

Formally, let $`s`$ denote a skill-style experience package and let
$`\mathcal{H}`$ denote a set of prior problem-solving trajectories. A
strategy gene is obtained by a distillation map

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
g=\psi(s)\quad\text{or}\quad g=\psi(\mathcal{H}),\qquad g\in\mathcal{G},
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(7)</span> |

where $`\psi`$ extracts a compact control-oriented representation from a
richer source experience object. At the representation level, we model a
gene as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
g=(m,u,\pi,\alpha,c,v),
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(8)</span> |

where $`m`$ denotes task-matching signals, $`u`$ a compact summary,
$`\pi`$ a small set of strategic steps, $`\alpha`$ a set of
failure-aware <span id="S3.SS3.p2.9.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">AVOID</span> cues, $`c`$ optional
execution constraints, and $`v`$ optional validation hooks. In our
operational setup, this corresponds to a compact template containing
keywords, summary, strategy, and <span id="S3.SS3.p2.9.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">AVOID</span> fields, with optional
constraint and validation metadata.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3.p3" class="ltx_para">

This structure differs from procedural skills in both organization and
purpose. Skills organize experience according to documentation logic;
genes organize experience according to control logic. Skills prioritize
readability and completeness; genes prioritize signal density,
applicability boundaries, and failure salience. The intended use is
therefore different as well: a procedural skill is primarily a
documentation artifact, whereas a strategy gene is a model-facing
control representation.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3.p4" class="ltx_para">

This distinction also explains why genes require protocolization.
Without a protocol layer, a gene remains a free-form prompt fragment
whose boundaries are unstable and whose internal fields are difficult to
compare, manipulate, or accumulate systematically. We therefore
introduce the *Gene Evolution Protocol* (GEP) as a representation
interface that canonicalizes genes into structured objects:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
\tilde{g}=\Gamma(g),\qquad\tilde{g}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(9)</span> |

where $`\Gamma`$ maps a raw gene instance into a canonical
protocol-compliant form.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3.p5" class="ltx_para">

In the GEP view, genes are the atomic capability units, while
higher-level objects such as capsules and events capture validated
execution paths and immutable evolution logs, respectively. Our focus in
this paper is the gene layer, because it is the minimal reusable unit
that can directly function as test-time control, while still supporting
composition and evolution under a shared protocol. A fuller
protocol-level formalization of GEP, including genes, capsules, events,
and the protocol loop, is provided in <a href="#A1" class="ltx_ref"
title="Appendix A Gene Evolution Protocol (GEP) ‣ From Procedural Skills to Strategy Genes: Towards Experience-Driven Test-Time Evolution"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Appendix</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">A</span></a>.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3.p6" class="ltx_para">

Canonicalization through GEP serves three purposes. First, it provides
stable boundaries and internal structure, making genes explicit objects
rather than ad hoc text snippets. Second, it makes genes comparable and
operable: once protocolized, genes can be matched, replaced, revised,
and composed at the object level. Third, it provides the minimal
interface required for accumulation and evolution, so that experience
units can be inherited, corrected, validated, and iteratively refined
over time. In this sense, GEP is not a formatting detail, but the
protocol layer that lifts a control-oriented representation into an
evolution-ready experience object. If procedural skills are
documentation artifacts, strategy genes under the GEP protocol are
evolution-ready control representations.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3.SSS1" class="section ltx_subsubsection">

#### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsubsection">3.3.1 </span>Experimental Settings

<div id="S3.SS3.SSS1.p1" class="ltx_para">

In this paper, *test-time control* refers to the ability of an
externalized experience representation to alter model behavior at
inference time without modifying model parameters. Formally, given a
task input $`x`$ and a reusable experience representation $`r`$, a fixed
model with parameters $`\theta`$ generates

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
y\sim p_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x,r;\gamma),
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(10)</span> |

where $`\gamma`$ denotes the inference configuration. In our
experiments, $`\gamma`$ is held fixed across conditions, using
low-temperature decoding ($`T=0.05`$), a maximum output length of
$`16{,}384`$ tokens, and a shared prompt injection interface.
Operationally, the task description is supplied separately from the
auxiliary control representation, allowing the latter to function as an
explicit test-time control signal.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3.SSS1.p2" class="ltx_para">

We focus on scientific tasks whose outputs are executable Python
programs evaluated in a sandbox. For trial $`i`$, let $`p_{i}`$ denote
the number of checkpoints passed by the generated program and $`n_{i}`$
the total number of checkpoints for that scenario. We define the
trial-level pass rate as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
r_{i}=\frac{p_{i}}{n_{i}},
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(11)</span> |

and report condition-level pass rate as the average trial-level score:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
\mathrm{PassRate}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}.
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(12)</span> |

A complete pass is additionally recorded when all checkpoints succeed,
i.e., $`p_{i}=n_{i}`$, but this is treated as a secondary descriptive
statistic rather than the primary metric. Each trial is executed under a
timeout of $`120`$ seconds.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3.SSS1.p3" class="ltx_para">

All experiments use two fixed Gemini models, Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview
(Pro) and Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite Preview (Flash), and comprise 4,590
retained trials in total. “Avg.” denotes the arithmetic mean over Pro
and Flash. $`\Delta`$ is computed against the no-guidance baseline
within this experiment, in percentage points. As shown
in <a href="#S3.F2" class="ltx_ref"
title="In 3.3.1 Experimental Settings ‣ 3.3 Strategy Genes and the GEP Protocol ‣ 3 Experience Representations for Test-Time Control ‣ From Procedural Skills to Strategy Genes: Towards Experience-Driven Test-Time Evolution"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Figure</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">2</span></a>, benchmark scenarios vary
substantially in checkpoint granularity, which is why we evaluate
conditions using checkpoint-based pass rate rather than binary trial
success alone. Representative tasks include protein parsing, UV-Vis
spectroscopy peak detection, exoplanet transit analysis, earthquake
catalog processing, climate attribution, community detection, and
inventory reordering. We provide the detailed settings
in <a href="#A2" class="ltx_ref"
title="Appendix B Detailed Experimental Setup ‣ From Procedural Skills to Strategy Genes: Towards Experience-Driven Test-Time Evolution"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Appendix</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">B</span></a>.

</div>

#### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsubsection">3.3.2 </span>Running Example

<div id="S3.SS3.SSS2.p1" class="ltx_para">

We use <span id="S3.SS3.SSS2.p1.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">S012_uv_spectroscopy</span> as a
running example. The task requires generating a Python program that
reads UV-Vis spectra from CSV files, detects peaks, computes peak
attributes such as wavelength, height, FWHM, and area, identifies the
dominant peak for each sample, and writes structured outputs.
Representative checkpoints cover command-line interface correctness,
peak detection, wavelength and height extraction, FWHM and area
computation, dominant-peak identification, and output structure.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3.SSS2.p2" class="ltx_para">

This scenario is particularly suitable for illustrating test-time
control because several recurring errors arise not from missing
high-level task understanding, but from incorrect operationalization of
library behavior. Two representative failure modes are to treat
<span id="S3.SS3.SSS2.p2.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">min_distance</span> as a wavelength
value rather than converting it into sample-index units before calling
<span id="S3.SS3.SSS2.p2.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">scipy.signal.find_peaks</span>, and
to use the raw output of <span id="S3.SS3.SSS2.p2.1.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">peak_widths</span> without
converting it back to wavelength units before reporting FWHM.

</div>

<div id="S3.SS3.SSS2.p3" class="ltx_para">

The corresponding <span id="S3.SS3.SSS2.p3.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span> representation
packages this experience in a documentation-oriented form, combining
task overview, workflow description, and auxiliary reference material.
The corresponding <span id="S3.SS3.SSS2.p3.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span> representation packages
the same underlying experience in a compact control-oriented form that
foregrounds a small number of high-risk decision points. A gene instance
is shown below.

</div>

Under checkpoint-based evaluation, these differences affect not only
whether the final program fully passes, but also which sub-tasks
succeed. For example, incorrect handling of
<span id="S3.SS3.SSS2.p5.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">min_distance</span> may reduce
peak-count accuracy while leaving file parsing and output formatting
intact, whereas incorrect conversion of <span id="S3.SS3.SSS2.p5.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">peak_widths</span> specifically
degrades FWHM-related checkpoints. This example illustrates the central
comparison of the paper: the same underlying experience can produce
different test-time control effects depending on whether it is packaged
for documentation or for behavioral guidance.

</div>

## <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_section">4 </span>Results and Analysis

<div id="S4.p1" class="ltx_para">

clear positive gain, whereas descriptive sections such as
<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p3.1.3"

reduce performance. The full <span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p3.1.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span> package also

usable signal is concentrated in workflow-style guidance.

</div>

<div id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p4" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p4.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter ltx_font_bold">Gene<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p4.1.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_serif"> is stronger than full
</span>Skill<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p4.1.1.2"

</span>Skill<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p4.1.1.3"

decomposition setting, <span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p4.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span> achieves the largest
gain overall, exceeding the full <span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p4.1.3"

<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p4.1.4"

<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p4.1.5"

<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p4.1.6"

<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p4.1.8"

</div>

<div id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p5" class="ltx_para">

Panel (b) then examines whether this advantage can be explained purely

</div>

<div id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p6" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p6.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Reducing
<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p6.1.1.1"

</div>

<div id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p7" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p7.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">The main
limitation of <span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p7.1.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span> lies not in missing
knowledge, but in how that knowledge is presented.</span>
<span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p7.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span> does contain useful

sparse and readily diluted by surrounding documentation-oriented
material. <span id="S4.SS1.SSS2.p7.1.3"

</div>

<span id="S4.SS2.SSS2.p4.1.3"

</div>

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">4.4 </span>Gene as a Carrier for Test-Time Evolution

<figure id="S4.F5" class="ltx_figure">
<img src="/html/2604.15097/assets/x5.png" id="S4.F5.g1"
class="ltx_graphics ltx_centering ltx_img_landscape" width="660"
height="234" alt="Refer to caption" />
<figcaption><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_figure"><span id="S4.F5.10.4.1"
class="ltx_text" style="font-size:90%;">Figure 5</span>: </span><span
id="S4.F5.6.3" class="ltx_text" style="font-size:90%;"> Accuracy (%) on
the CritPt benchmark. Two gene-evolved systems, <span id="S4.F5.6.3.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver (Gene) 2026-02-16</span>
and <span id="S4.F5.6.3.2" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver
(Gene) 2026-03-26</span>, substantially outperform their paired base
models, Gemini 3 Pro Preview and Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview, by <span
class="math inline">+9.47</span> and <span
class="math inline">+9.44</span> percentage points, respectively. The
stronger evolved version reaches <span class="math inline">27.14%</span>
accuracy, illustrating that gene-based test-time evolution can yield
substantial gains beyond the underlying base model. </span></figcaption>
</figure>

<div id="S4.SS4.p1" class="ltx_para">

We next ask a stronger question than one-shot test-time control: can
*Gene* also serve as a carrier for *test-time evolution*, enabling
iterative improvement across runs without changing the underlying base
model? To study this, we construct an evolutionary agent using
OpenClaw<span id="footnote1"
class="ltx_note ltx_role_footnote"><sup>1</sup><span class="ltx_note_outer"><span class="ltx_note_content"><sup>1</sup><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_note">1</span><a href="https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw"
class="ltx_ref ltx_url ltx_font_typewriter"
target="_blank">https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw</a></span></span></span>
as the host runtime and Evolver<span id="footnote2"
class="ltx_note ltx_role_footnote"><sup>2</sup><span class="ltx_note_outer"><span class="ltx_note_content"><sup>2</sup><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_note">2</span><a href="https://github.com/EvoMap/evolver"
class="ltx_ref ltx_url ltx_font_typewriter"
target="_blank">https://github.com/EvoMap/evolver</a></span></span></span>
as the evolution engine. At a high level, Evolver organizes
self-evolution around structured Genes, accumulates causal experience
through memory, and solidifies successful updates through
validation-governed evolution events. As shown
in <a href="#S4.F5" class="ltx_ref"
title="In 4.4 Gene as a Carrier for Test-Time Evolution ‣ 4 Results and Analysis ‣ From Procedural Skills to Strategy Genes: Towards Experience-Driven Test-Time Evolution"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Figure</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">5</span></a>, both evolved agents
substantially outperform their paired base models on CritPt
benchmark \[<span class="ltx_ref ltx_missing_citation ltx_ref_self">CritPt</span>\]:
<span id="S4.SS4.p1.4.3" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver
(Gene) 2026-02-16</span> reaches $`18.57\%`$ versus $`9.1\%`$ for Gemini
3 Pro Preview, while <span id="S4.SS4.p1.4.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver (Gene) 2026-03-26</span>
reaches $`27.14\%`$ versus $`17.7\%`$ for Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview.
Details are deferred to <a href="#A4" class="ltx_ref"
title="Appendix D Additional Details on Gene-Driven Test-Time Evolution ‣ From Procedural Skills to Strategy Genes: Towards Experience-Driven Test-Time Evolution"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Appendix</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">D</span></a>.

</div>

<div id="S4.SS4.p2" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="S4.SS4.p2.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Version A:
<span id="S4.SS4.p2.1.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver
(Gene) 2026-02-16</span>.</span> We view the earlier version as
*memory-grounded* evolution: its gains mainly come from consolidating
prior failures, execution traces, and corrective experience into
reusable control units. A representative case is
<span id="S4.SS4.p2.1.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">gene_gep_repair_from_errors</span>,
which is triggered by explicit failure signals such as
<span id="S4.SS4.p2.1.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">error</span>,
<span id="S4.SS4.p2.1.5"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">exception</span>,
<span id="S4.SS4.p2.1.6"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">failed</span>, and
<span id="S4.SS4.p2.1.7"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">unstable</span>, and follows a
tightly constrained repair loop: structured diagnosis, blast-radius
estimation, smallest reversible patch, validation, and solidification
back into the Gene/Capsule store. The key point is that the gene does
not merely remind the agent to “be careful.” It packages a reusable
repair procedure. Once distilled, the same pattern can be invoked again
in later runs instead of being rediscovered from scratch. This is the
first sense in which gene iteration is useful: it converts past failures
into reusable future-time control.

</div>

<div id="S4.SS4.p3" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="S4.SS4.p3.7.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Version B:
<span id="S4.SS4.p3.7.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver
(Gene) 2026-03-26</span>.</span> The later version reflects a broader,
*exploration-augmented* regime. Across $`70`$ tasks, the system uses
$`210`$ gene slots and $`36`$ unique gene IDs, with
<span id="S4.SS4.p3.7.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">arxiv</span>-derived genes
accounting for $`148`$ selections, compared with $`44`$ from
<span id="S4.SS4.p3.7.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">run_experience</span> and $`18`$
from <span id="S4.SS4.p3.7.5"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">builtin_topic_prior</span>. The
most revealing case is <span id="S4.SS4.p3.7.6"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">gene_topic_hamiltonian_inverse_design</span>,
the most frequent high-value run-experience gene in the full run,
selected $`25`$ times. Its content is already strongly procedural:
enumerate constraints such as commutation, normalization, and operator
ordering; construct coefficients under those constraints; decompose
many-body-chain problems into local terms while preserving index
consistency; and prioritize numerical stability and reproducibility,
ideally with symbolic and numerical checking. This shows what gene
iteration preserves at a later stage: not just memories of failure, but
compact task-facing solution procedures. Its practical value is visible
in repeated high-frequency combinations, especially the recurring trio
<span id="S4.SS4.p3.7.7"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">gene_topic_hamiltonian_inverse_design +
gene_topic_many_body_spin_chain +
gene_topic_seed_many_body_spin_chain</span>, which appears across 12
tasks. This is the second sense in which gene iteration is useful: the
system is no longer only repairing itself, but also accumulating
directly reusable solution patterns and deploying them repeatedly at
scale.

</div>

<div id="S4.SS4.p4" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="S4.SS4.p4.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Discussion.</span> Taken together, the
two versions show that gene-based evolution is not merely becoming
deeper, but broader. <span id="S4.SS4.p4.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver (Gene) 2026-02-16</span>
mainly consolidates failures into stable repair and validation loops,
whereas <span id="S4.SS4.p4.1.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver (Gene) 2026-03-26</span>
further converts successful task experience into reusable domain-facing
procedures. This distinction is central to our overall argument. If a
reusable experience unit is to support test-time evolution, it must
remain compact enough for inference-time injection, structured enough
for targeted update, and practical enough to preserve procedures that
continue to work in later problems. The CritPt results provide concrete
evidence for this view: Gene is not only a control-oriented
representation for one-shot inference, but also a persistent
optimization interface through which reusable experience can accumulate,
improve, and become increasingly useful through iteration.

</div>

</div>

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">4.5 </span>Overall Findings

<div id="S4.SS5.p1" class="ltx_para">

The retained analyses support four main findings.

</div>

<div id="S4.SS5.p2" class="ltx_para">

- <span id="S4.I1.i1"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="S4.I1.i1.p1" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

  <span id="S4.I1.i1.p1.1.1"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Documentation-oriented skills are
  misaligned with test-time control.</span> The useful control signal
  inside <span id="S4.I1.i1.p1.1.2"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span> is sparse and
  concentrated in a narrow procedural slice. Expanding a compact
  experience object into a fuller documentation package does not
  reliably help and can degrade the overall average.

  </div>
- <span id="S4.I1.i2"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="S4.I1.i2.p1" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

  <span id="S4.I1.i2.p1.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Strategy
  genes are better understood as control-oriented experience objects
  rather than merely shorter prompts.</span> The benefit of
  <span id="S4.I1.i2.p1.1.2"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span> does not follow a
  simple token-budget trend, but emerges when experience is organized
  into strategy. Matched-budget <span id="S4.I1.i2.p1.1.3"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span> fragments narrow the
  gap but do not reverse the conclusion, and reattaching
  documentation-oriented material usually weakens rather than improves
  <span id="S4.I1.i2.p1.1.4"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span>.

  </div>
- <span id="S4.I1.i3"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="S4.I1.i3.p1" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

  <span id="S4.I1.i3.p1.1.1"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter ltx_font_bold">Gene<span id="S4.I1.i3.p1.1.1.1"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_serif"> provides a better substrate for
  experience accumulation, but accumulation should be selective rather
  than additive.</span></span> Attached failure history is more
  effective in <span id="S4.I1.i3.p1.1.2"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span> than in
  <span id="S4.I1.i3.p1.1.3"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span> or freeform text,
  and editable structure matters beyond content alone. At the same time,
  naively appending additional failure text can dilute an otherwise
  effective control object.

  </div>
- <span id="S4.I1.i4"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="S4.I1.i4.p1" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

  <span id="S4.I1.i4.p1.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Failure
  experience is most useful when distilled into compact warnings, and
  gene-based evolution can accumulate increasingly useful strategy
  objects over time.</span> Within <span id="S4.I1.i4.p1.1.2"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span>, compact failure
  warnings outperform mixed strategy–failure bundles, indicating that
  accumulation works best through selective compression rather than
  additive growth. Beyond one-shot control, gene-evolved systems also
  achieve substantial gains over their paired base models, showing that

<div id="S5" class="section ltx_section">

## <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_section">5 </span>Conclusion

<div id="S5.p1" class="ltx_para">

This paper studies a representational question in experience reuse: not
how to provide more prior experience at test time, but how to encode
reusable experience so that it functions as effective control under
inference constraints. Across controlled experiments, we show that
documentation-oriented <span id="S5.p1.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span> objects are poorly
aligned with test-time control: their useful signal is sparse and often
diluted by surrounding documentation-oriented material. Against this
background, we introduce <span id="S5.p1.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span> as a compact
control-oriented experience representation. The retained analyses show
that its advantage is not reducible to shorter prompting alone. Rather,
<span id="S5.p1.1.3" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span>
provides a more effective interface for organizing strategy, preserving
task-relevant control, and supporting bounded reuse. Beyond one-shot
control, we further show that <span id="S5.p1.1.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span> is a better substrate
for iterative experience accumulation. Attached failure history is more
effective in <span id="S5.p1.1.5"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span> than in
<span id="S5.p1.1.6" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span>
or freeform text, and failure information is most useful when distilled
into compact warnings rather than naively appended. Across two
evolutionary runs on CritPt, gene-evolved systems substantially
outperform their paired base models, suggesting that compact and
revisable experience objects can support iterative improvement over
time. More broadly, our results suggest that experience reuse in
LLM-based systems should be studied at the level of representation
design rather than prompt packaging alone. We hope this work motivates
further research on control-oriented experience objects, bounded reuse,
and evolution-ready representations for test-time adaptation.

</div>

<div id="A1.p1" class="ltx_para">

We provide a formalization of the Gene Evolution Protocol (GEP) used in
this work. As shown in <a href="#A1.T8" class="ltx_ref"
title="In Appendix A Gene Evolution Protocol (GEP) ‣ From Procedural Skills to Strategy Genes: Towards Experience-Driven Test-Time Evolution"><span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Table</span>˜<span
class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">8</span></a>, consistent with the public
GEP description, we treat the protocol as an object layer for reusable
experience rather than as a mere prompt format or logging convention. At
a high level, GEP organizes experience into three object types—*genes*,
*capsules*, and *events*—and updates them through a
trial–validation–solidification loop. In the Gene-Bench setting used in
this paper, the gene layer is the primary object of study, while
capsules and events define the audit and evolution interfaces around it.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS1" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">A.1 </span>Protocol Scope and Design Philosophy

<div id="A1.SS1.p1" class="ltx_para">

GEP is designed to support three requirements that free-form prompt
fragments do not satisfy reliably. First, reusable experience should
have stable boundaries and a canonical internal structure, so that it
can be analyzed and reused as an explicit object rather than as
unstructured text. Second, experience objects should be comparable and
operable: they should admit matching, replacement, revision,
composition, and validation. Third, experience should remain evolvable
over time, meaning that successful adaptations can be accumulated,
inherited, corrected, and audited rather than merely replayed as one-off
prompt patches.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS1.p2" class="ltx_para">

Under this view, GEP is not introduced to make prompting more elaborate.
It is introduced to make reusable experience *object-like*: explicit,
structured, and protocolized enough to support lifecycle management at
test time and across repeated task episodes.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A1.SS2" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">A.2 </span>Object Hierarchy

<div id="A1.SS2.p1" class="ltx_para">

GEP defines three object layers:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{G}\cup\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{E},
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(13)</span> |

where $`\mathcal{G}`$ denotes the set of genes, $`\mathcal{C}`$ the set
of capsules, and $`\mathcal{E}`$ the set of events.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS2.p2" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS2.p2.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Genes.</span> A
*gene* is the atomic capability unit. It is the smallest reusable
experience representation that can directly function as test-time
control. In this paper, genes are compact strategy templates distilled
from richer experience sources such as procedural skills or prior
trajectories.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS2.p3" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS2.p3.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Capsules.</span>
A *capsule* is a validated task-level execution path with audit trail.
Whereas a gene encodes an abstract reusable strategy, a capsule records
how one or more genes are instantiated and validated in a concrete
task-solving episode. Capsules therefore operate at a higher granularity
than genes: they capture successful compositions, execution context, and
validation outcomes.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS2.p4" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS2.p4.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Events.</span>
An *event* is an immutable evolution log. Events record
protocol-relevant state transitions, such as mutation, repair,
validation failure, validation success, or solidification. Their role is
not to serve as direct control input, but to preserve provenance,
lineage, and auditability of experience evolution.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS2.p5" class="ltx_para">

This hierarchy induces the following functional distinction:

- <span id="A1.I1.i1"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I1.i1.p1" class="ltx_para">

  <span id="A1.I1.i1.p1.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">gene</span>:
  control unit

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I1.i2"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I1.i2.p1" class="ltx_para">

  <span id="A1.I1.i2.p1.1.1"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">capsule</span>: validated execution
  unit

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I1.i3"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I1.i3.p1" class="ltx_para">

  <span id="A1.I1.i3.p1.1.1"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">event</span>: evolution record

  </div>

</div>

</div>

<div id="A1.SS3" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">A.3 </span>Gene Objects

<div id="A1.SS3.p1" class="ltx_para">

A gene is a compact, control-oriented representation distilled from
prior experience:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
g=\psi(z),\qquad z\in\{s,\mathcal{H},\mathcal{C}\},\quad g\in\mathcal{G},
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(14)</span> |

where $`z`$ may be a procedural skill $`s`$, a set of trajectories
$`\mathcal{H}`$, or a previously validated capsule $`\mathcal{C}`$.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS3.p2" class="ltx_para">

For the purposes of this paper, we formalize a gene as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
g=(m,u,\pi,\alpha,c,v,\eta),
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(15)</span> |

where

- <span id="A1.I2.i1"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I2.i1.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`m`$ denotes task-matching signals (e.g., keywords or trigger cues),

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I2.i2"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I2.i2.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`u`$ denotes a compact summary of the intended behavior,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I2.i3"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I2.i3.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`\pi`$ denotes a small set of strategic steps,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I2.i4"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I2.i4.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`\alpha`$ denotes failure-aware <span id="A1.I2.i4.p1.1.1"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">AVOID</span> cues,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I2.i5"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I2.i5.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`c`$ denotes optional execution constraints,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I2.i6"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I2.i6.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`v`$ denotes validation hooks or executable checks,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I2.i7"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I2.i7.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`\eta`$ denotes canonical metadata such as type, version, identifier,
  and hash.

  </div>

</div>

<div id="A1.SS3.p3" class="ltx_para">

This abstraction matches the operational schema used in our Gene-Bench
setup, where a gene is serialized with fields such as
<span id="A1.SS3.p3.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">type</span>,
<span id="A1.SS3.p3.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">schema_version</span>,
<span id="A1.SS3.p3.1.3" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">id</span>,
<span id="A1.SS3.p3.1.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">signals_match</span>,
<span id="A1.SS3.p3.1.5"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">summary</span>,
<span id="A1.SS3.p3.1.6"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">strategy</span>,
<span id="A1.SS3.p3.1.7"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">constraints</span>,
<span id="A1.SS3.p3.1.8"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">validation</span>, and
<span id="A1.SS3.p3.1.9"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">asset_id</span>. A representative
example is a <span id="A1.SS3.p3.1.10"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">\<strategy-gene\></span> block
containing domain keywords, a one-sentence summary, a short strategy
list, and one or more <span id="A1.SS3.p3.1.11"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">AVOID</span> items. In our
experiments, such genes are typically around $`230`$ tokens and are
designed to provide control-relevant experience under limited inference
budget.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A1.SS4" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">A.4 </span>Capsule Objects

<div id="A1.SS4.p1" class="ltx_para">

A capsule records a validated task-solving path that can serve as a
higher-level reusable asset. Unlike a gene, which abstracts a compact
control strategy, a capsule preserves the successful realization of a
strategy in context. For clarity, we formalize a capsule in this paper
as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
\kappa=(q,G_{\kappa},T_{\kappa},o_{\kappa},V_{\kappa},\ell_{\kappa}),\qquad\kappa\in\mathcal{C},
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(16)</span> |

where

- <span id="A1.I3.i1"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I3.i1.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`q`$ is a task signature or problem context,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I3.i2"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I3.i2.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`G_{\kappa}\subseteq\mathcal{G}`$ is the set of genes used or
  instantiated,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I3.i3"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I3.i3.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`T_{\kappa}`$ is the execution trace or action path,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I3.i4"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I3.i4.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`o_{\kappa}`$ is the observed outcome,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I3.i5"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I3.i5.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`V_{\kappa}`$ is the validation record or audit trail,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I3.i6"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I3.i6.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`\ell_{\kappa}`$ is the lineage pointer linking the capsule to prior
  events or parent assets.

  </div>

</div>

<div id="A1.SS4.p2" class="ltx_para">

This paper-side formalization is consistent with the public GEP
description, in which capsules correspond to successful task execution
paths and serve as validated assets above the gene layer. Conceptually,
a capsule answers the question: *How was a control strategy actually
realized and validated in a concrete task episode?*

</div>

</div>

<div id="A1.SS5" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">A.5 </span>Event Objects

<div id="A1.SS5.p1" class="ltx_para">

Events are immutable records of protocol-relevant changes. They preserve
provenance and make evolution auditable. We formalize an event as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
e=(t,\rho,a_{\mathrm{src}},a_{\mathrm{dst}},\sigma,\iota,\Delta,\nu,\tau),\qquad e\in\mathcal{E},
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(17)</span> |

where

- <span id="A1.I4.i1"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I4.i1.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`t`$ is an event type (e.g., <span id="A1.I4.i1.p1.1.1"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">repair</span>,
  <span id="A1.I4.i1.p1.1.2"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">innovation</span>,
  <span id="A1.I4.i1.p1.1.3"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">validation_pass</span>,
  <span id="A1.I4.i1.p1.1.4"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">validation_fail</span>,
  <span id="A1.I4.i1.p1.1.5"
  class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">solidify</span>),

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I4.i2"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I4.i2.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`\rho`$ is the parent run or episode identifier,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I4.i3"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I4.i3.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`a_{\mathrm{src}}`$ and $`a_{\mathrm{dst}}`$ denote source and target
  assets,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I4.i4"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I4.i4.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`\sigma`$ is the triggering signal,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I4.i5"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I4.i5.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`\iota`$ is the intended mutation or repair objective,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I4.i6"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I4.i6.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`\Delta`$ is the proposed change or diff,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I4.i7"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I4.i7.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`\nu`$ is the validation result,

  </div>
- <span id="A1.I4.i8"><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_item">•</span></span>
  <div id="A1.I4.i8.p1" class="ltx_para">

  $`\tau`$ is the timestamp or ordering key.

  </div>

</div>

<div id="A1.SS5.p2" class="ltx_para">

This formalization instantiates the GEP view that events are immutable
evolution logs recording each mutation or repair with associated
context. Events are therefore not direct control representations; they
are the protocol substrate for traceability, auditability, and lineage
reconstruction.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A1.SS6" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">A.6 </span>The GEP Loop

<div id="A1.SS6.p1" class="ltx_para">

The protocol updates experience objects through a six-stage loop:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | 
``` math
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})\xrightarrow{\textsc{scan}}\sigma\xrightarrow{\textsc{signal}}\hat{\sigma}\xrightarrow{\textsc{intent}}\iota\xrightarrow{\textsc{mutate}}a^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\textsc{validate}}\nu\xrightarrow{\textsc{solidify}}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}),
``` |  | <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_equation ltx_align_right">(18)</span> |

where $`a^{\prime}`$ denotes a candidate asset, typically a new or
revised gene (and optionally an associated capsule), and $`\nu`$ is the
validation outcome.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS6.p2" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS6.p2.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Scan.</span>
Runtime traces, tool logs, execution failures, or stagnation signals are
monitored.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS6.p3" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS6.p3.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Signal.</span>
Raw traces are converted into standardized protocol signals. This step
turns unstructured execution evidence into machine-actionable mutation
or repair triggers.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS6.p4" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS6.p4.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Intent.</span>
The protocol determines the objective of evolution, such as repair,
optimization, or extension.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS6.p5" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS6.p5.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Mutate.</span> A
candidate asset is generated by rewriting code, prompt structure,
strategy steps, <span id="A1.SS6.p5.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">AVOID</span> cues, or associated
metadata.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS6.p6" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS6.p6.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Validate.</span>
The candidate is executed in a sandbox or otherwise checked against
validation hooks. Only assets that satisfy the required criteria are
eligible for persistence.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS6.p7" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS6.p7.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Solidify.</span>
Validated capability is written back into the protocolized repository as
a new or revised gene, and the corresponding capsule and event records
are updated.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS6.p8" class="ltx_para">

This loop is the protocol-level analogue of
trial–validation–solidification. Its function is not merely to repair a
single run, but to convert transient runtime adaptation into persistent
reusable experience.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A1.SS7" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">A.7 </span>Canonicalization and Protocol Invariants

<div id="A1.SS7.p1" class="ltx_para">

For protocolization to be meaningful, experience objects must satisfy
several invariants.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS7.p2" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS7.p2.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Stable
boundaries.</span> A gene must have explicit fields and canonical
serialization, so that it can be identified as an object rather than
treated as free-form text.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS7.p3" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS7.p3.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Control-oriented
structure.</span> A gene must encode compact control-relevant content,
rather than documentation-heavy explanation. In our setting, this
typically takes the form of matching signals, summary, strategy, and
<span id="A1.SS7.p3.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">AVOID</span> cues.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS7.p4" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS7.p4.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Operability.</span> Protocolized genes
must support matching, replacement, revision, and composition.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS7.p5" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS7.p5.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Validatability.</span> A gene or capsule
must expose executable or otherwise explicit validation interfaces.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS7.p6" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A1.SS7.p6.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Lineage and
auditability.</span> Capsules and events must preserve enough provenance
information to reconstruct how an experience object was produced,
validated, and modified.

</div>

<div id="A1.SS7.p7" class="ltx_para">

These invariants explain why GEP should be viewed as a protocol layer
rather than a formatting convention. Canonicalization is what turns a
useful prompt fragment into an analyzable, composable, and evolvable
experience object.

</div>

</div>

</div>

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">B.4 </span>Control Representations and Injection Interface

<div id="A2.SS4.p1" class="ltx_para">

We distinguish two main control representations in the retained
analyses: <span id="A2.SS4.p1.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span> and
<span id="A2.SS4.p1.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span>.
<span id="A2.SS4.p1.1.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Gene</span> is the compact strategy
representation used throughout the main comparisons and is approximately
230 tokens long in the retained setting. <span id="A2.SS4.p1.1.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span> denotes the full
documentation-style skill package, including the main skill document
together with auxiliary scripts, notes, and references, and is
approximately 2,500 tokens long. A no-guidance condition is also
included as the baseline, in which no auxiliary control prompt is
injected.

</div>

<div id="A2.SS4.p2" class="ltx_para">

Across all conditions, the task specification is held fixed at the
scenario level, while the control representation varies by experimental
condition. The task description is supplied through the user-facing
<span id="A2.SS4.p2.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">contents</span> field, and any
auxiliary control context is injected separately through
<span id="A2.SS4.p2.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">systemInstruction</span>. This
separation ensures that comparisons are driven by differences in control
representation rather than by rewriting the underlying task itself.

</div>

<div id="A2.SS4.p3" class="ltx_para">

Gene conditions use a compact, strategy-oriented control representation.
In the retained experiments, the injected Gene content is serialized as
a structured GEP-style strategy object and primarily contains concise
strategic cues, such as domain-relevant signals, a short summary, and
core strategy steps, rather than full documentation. Skill conditions
use a documentation-oriented control representation, in which the full
skill package is provided as external guidance for the same underlying
task. Some retained analyses further place the Gene representation
inside an evolution-style contextual wrapper, allowing us to study
whether failure-aware organization and iterative experience framing
change its effectiveness.

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">C.1 </span>Decomposing <span id="A3.SS1.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Skill</span> into Sections

<div id="A3.SS1.p1" class="ltx_para">

<a href="#A3.T12" class="ltx_ref"

class="ltx_text ltx_ref_tag">Table</span>˜<span

<span id="A3.SS1.p1.1.3"

incorrect content is harmless, but because some mutations preserve the

## <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_appendix">Appendix D </span>Additional Details on Gene-Driven Test-Time Evolution

<div id="A4.p1" class="ltx_para">

We designed two evolution tasks on top of OpenClaw<span id="footnote3"
class="ltx_note ltx_role_footnote"><sup>3</sup><span class="ltx_note_outer"><span class="ltx_note_content"><sup>3</sup><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_note">3</span><a href="https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw"
class="ltx_ref ltx_url ltx_font_typewriter"
target="_blank">https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw</a></span></span></span>
and Evolver<span id="footnote4"
class="ltx_note ltx_role_footnote"><sup>4</sup><span class="ltx_note_outer"><span class="ltx_note_content"><sup>4</sup><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_note">4</span><a href="https://github.com/EvoMap/evolver"
class="ltx_ref ltx_url ltx_font_typewriter"
target="_blank">https://github.com/EvoMap/evolver</a></span></span></span>,
and let the resulting evolutionary agent run for approximately two days
in each version. The two tasks were intentionally different. The first
task targeted *memory-grounded* self-improvement: the goal was to
stabilize execution by repeatedly converting observed failures into
reusable repair and protocol assets. The second task targeted
*exploration-augmented* self-improvement: the goal was to expand the
usable gene pool by combining prior run experience with externally
sourced genes and topic-facing heuristics. This setting matches
Evolver’s public framing of *Exploration mode* as moving beyond
single-loop self-repair toward proactive acquisition of new genes.

</div>

<div id="A4.SS1" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">D.1 </span>Evolution Task Design for <span id="A4.SS1.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver (Gene) 2026-02-16</span>

<div id="A4.SS1.p1" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A4.SS1.p1.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Task
objective.</span> For <span id="A4.SS1.p1.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver (Gene) 2026-02-16</span>,
we designed an evolution task centered on *stabilization through
memory*. The agent was not asked to broadly search for new capabilities.
Instead, it was asked to improve by looking inward: inspect prior logs,
identify recurring execution failures and bottlenecks, and distill those
observations into reusable genes. The core hypothesis was that a useful
evolution carrier should first be able to preserve and replay corrective
experience, rather than merely produce one-off fixes.

</div>

<div id="A4.SS1.p2" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A4.SS1.p2.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Evolution
mechanism.</span> Within this setting, Evolver acts as a structured
evolution engine rather than a free-form prompting layer. It selects or
updates genes based on runtime signals, constrains candidate
modifications through explicit strategy fields, and requires validation
before successful experience is solidified. As a result, the evolved
agent does not simply “remember more” in a loose sense. Instead, it
gradually accumulates compact procedures that can be reinvoked in later
runs.

</div>

<div id="A4.SS1.p3" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A4.SS1.p3.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Why this version
is memory-grounded.</span> The first version is memory-grounded because
the dominant improvement signal comes from prior failures and internal
traces. The agent evolves by repeatedly compressing observed breakdowns
into reusable defensive procedures. This makes the resulting gains
structurally conservative but highly practical: the system becomes more
stable not by adding broad new knowledge, but by turning past errors
into future-time control.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A4.SS2" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">D.2 </span>Expanded Cases for <span id="A4.SS2.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver (Gene) 2026-02-16</span>

<div id="A4.SS2.p1" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A4.SS2.p1.3.4" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Case A1:
<span id="A4.SS2.p1.3.4.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">gene_gep_repair_from_errors</span>.</span>
This gene is the clearest example of how the first version turns failure
memory into reusable control. It is triggered by explicit error-related
signals such as <span id="A4.SS2.p1.3.5"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">error</span>,
<span id="A4.SS2.p1.3.6"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">exception</span>,
<span id="A4.SS2.p1.3.7"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">failed</span>, and
<span id="A4.SS2.p1.3.8"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">unstable</span>. Its strategy is
procedural rather than rhetorical: (1) extract structured signals from
logs and instructions; (2) select an existing matching Gene instead of
improvising; (3) estimate blast radius before editing; (4) apply the
smallest reversible patch; (5) validate the result; and (6) solidify the
successful pattern back into the Gene/Capsule store. This is important
because the gene does not merely advise the agent to “debug carefully.”
It stores a reusable repair loop: *structured diagnosis $`\rightarrow`$
constrained patching $`\rightarrow`$ validation $`\rightarrow`$
solidification*. Once this loop is encoded as a gene, later runs can
invoke it directly rather than rediscovering it from scratch.

</div>

<div id="A4.SS2.p2" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A4.SS2.p2.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Case A2:
<span id="A4.SS2.p2.1.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">gene_gep_innovate_from_opportunity</span>.</span>
A complementary case is <span id="A4.SS2.p2.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">gene_gep_innovate_from_opportunity</span>,
which shows that the first version does not only repair failures, but
also internalizes useful opportunity signals. This gene is matched to
signals such as <span id="A4.SS2.p2.1.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">user_feature_request</span>,
<span id="A4.SS2.p2.1.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">user_improvement_suggestion</span>,
<span id="A4.SS2.p2.1.5"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">perf_bottleneck</span>,
<span id="A4.SS2.p2.1.6"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">capability_gap</span>, and
<span id="A4.SS2.p2.1.7"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">stable_success_plateau</span>. Its
strategy is again strongly procedural: extract opportunity signals,
search existing Genes and Capsules to avoid reinvention, design a
minimal and testable implementation plan, estimate blast radius,
validate the change, and finally solidify the new pattern as a Gene or
Capsule if it proves useful. Compared with the repair gene above, this
gene is less reactive and more forward-looking. However, it still
remains memory-grounded in the sense that it derives new structure from
accumulated runtime observations rather than from broad external
exploration. Together, Cases A1 and A2 show why gene iteration is
already useful in the first version: it preserves both defensive repair
routines and internally discovered improvement routines as reusable
evolution assets.

</div>

</div>

<div id="A4.SS3" class="section ltx_subsection">

### <span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_subsection">D.3 </span>Evolution Task Design for <span id="A4.SS3.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver (Gene) 2026-03-26</span>

<div id="A4.SS3.p1" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A4.SS3.p1.1.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Task
objective.</span> For <span id="A4.SS3.p1.1.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">Evolver (Gene) 2026-03-26</span>,
we designed the evolution task in the spirit of Evolver’s *Exploration
mode*. The goal was no longer only to consolidate existing repair
knowledge, but to broaden the evolution substrate by acquiring and
routing new task-relevant genes. Concretely, this meant combining
previously accumulated run experience with externally sourced genes,
especially paper-derived and topic-facing assets, so that the agent
could learn not only from past failures but also from possible future
solution patterns. The generated answers are publicly available
at <a href="https://github.com/EvoMap/critpt-openclaw-reproducible-70"
class="ltx_ref ltx_url ltx_font_typewriter"
target="_blank">https://github.com/EvoMap/critpt-openclaw-reproducible-70</a>.

</div>

<div id="A4.SS3.p2" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A4.SS3.p2.1.1"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Exploration-oriented mechanism.</span>
This version still keeps the base model fixed. The change happens at the
experience layer. The agent is allowed to expand beyond the internal
repair loop by selecting from a larger gene pool and routing different
genes to different tasks. In this sense, the second task is not merely a
larger version of the first. It reflects a different evolutionary
regime: instead of asking how to make the current agent more stable
within known behaviors, it asks how to enlarge the set of reusable
procedures available to the agent.

</div>

<div id="A4.SS3.p3" class="ltx_para ltx_noindent">

<span id="A4.SS3.p3.6.1" class="ltx_text ltx_font_bold">Full-run
statistics.</span> The resulting run covers $`70`$ tasks with $`210`$
gene slots and $`36`$ unique gene IDs. The source distribution is highly
asymmetric: <span id="A4.SS3.p3.6.2"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">arxiv</span>-derived genes account
for $`148`$ selections, compared with $`44`$ from
<span id="A4.SS3.p3.6.3"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">run_experience</span> and $`18`$
from <span id="A4.SS3.p3.6.4"
class="ltx_text ltx_font_typewriter">builtin_topic_prior</span>. This
shows that the second version is not simply replaying a small internal
memory store. It is operating with a broadened experience substrate in
which external knowledge plays a major role.

</div>

</div>
